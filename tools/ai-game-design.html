Method

Across three games I gave Codex more say, from implementing a brief to helping find a premise. I used design notes and Git snapshots to trace what survived my playtests.

Experiments

Best played on desktop.

01 / Delegation

No Signal

I designed and produced the puzzle around the constraints of my own desk, with limited ports, cable reach, and budget. I used the first level to teach power, then introduced a hub when the laptop ran out of ports.

Codex built movement, connections, and device feedback in stages. When it asked what USB-C could carry, we specified the power, video, and data rules before expanding the puzzles.

How power propagates

isPlugEnergized() tracks visited endpoints to stop cycles. getPoweredHubDevices() adds reachable hub outputs until no new hub gets power.

02 / Collaboration

Catwalk

I led design and playtesting, starting with my cat Mocha’s habit of stepping on the keyboard. I tried different key layouts for moves like squeezing through a gap or rolling across one.

Codex built the Phaser prototype and 25 test levels. The obstacles rarely worked together, so I asked Codex to build an editor. I authored one continuous route, and we made routes shareable as JSON.

03 / Direction

Two Minds 二心

I directed the game through playtesting and made the final design calls. Codex led the concept interviews and built prototypes around possession and ritual. We cut early combat when it felt disconnected from the ritual.

I brought in the rival Wukongs from Journey to the West. Codex built an auto-battle prototype on a hex board where the double fights with the traits you give up.

Field handbook

Delegation Write a passing and a failing case

A cable connects within reach. Move the device farther away and the cable disconnects. Ask Codex to build both cases together.

Collaboration Change one thing per playtest

Keep the route fixed and change when Mocha blocks a key. Save both builds alongside your playtest notes.

Direction Ask a question before building

Ask Codex to find what draws you to an idea. Name one thing you want to learn, then build the smallest test you can play.

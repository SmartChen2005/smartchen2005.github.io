Method

Across three games I gave Codex more say, from implementing a brief to helping find a premise. I used design notes and Git snapshots to trace what survived my playtests.

Experiments

Best played on desktop.

01 / Delegation

No Signal

I designed and produced a puzzle based on wiring my own desk. I set limits on ports, cable reach, and budget, then planned levels where each new device could upset a wiring plan that had worked before.

I handed a complete design brief to Codex. Its questions exposed gaps in the brief, including what USB-C could carry. We clarified those rules and tested movement, wiring, and device feedback in stages as Codex built them.

02 / Collaboration

Catwalk

My cat Mocha used to step on my keyboard while I worked. I gave Codex that premise and asked it to choose the first prototype. It built the platformer in Phaser. I playtested moves and key layouts while Mocha interfered with the controls.

In Codex’s 25 rooms, obstacles rarely worked together. I asked it to turn the rooms into templates and build an editor. I used it to design one continuous route. The JSON format opened the same build, playtest, and share loop to players.

03 / Direction

Two Minds 二心

I started with no genre or premise. I asked GPT, through Codex, to lead the search for a game I wanted to make. My job was to react to the proposals, play the prototypes, and decide what to keep.

The rule I kept coming back to was that a choice should change what you encounter later. In Journey to the West, the two Wukongs gave me a way to make that rule the game.

Play Two Minds {{arrow}}

How we found the concept

  1. Open with questions

    GPT explored one design tension at a time and recorded what I was drawn to, what I rejected, and what I was unsure about.

  2. Cut combat and inventory

    The possession and ritual prototypes accumulated combat, loot, and an inventory. I found the combat got in the way, so we cut that loop.

  3. Build the rival Wukong

    I brought in a new design based on the rival Wukongs. Codex built the hex-board auto-battle test, where the double inherits the traits you give up.

Field handbook

Delegation Write a passing and a failing case

A cable connects within reach. Move the device farther away and the cable disconnects. Ask Codex to build both cases together.

Collaboration Change one thing per playtest

Keep the route fixed and change when Mocha blocks a key. Save both builds alongside your playtest notes.

Direction Ask a question before building

Ask Codex to find what draws you to an idea. Name one thing you want to learn, then build the smallest test you can play.

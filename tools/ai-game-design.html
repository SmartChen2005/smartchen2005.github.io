Method

Who gets
to decide?

I started with a fixed navigation brief, then gave Codex more say over mechanics and the concept. Design notes and Git snapshots trace how my work shifted from specifying rules to playing prototypes and deciding what to keep.

One designer, three different games. A controlled comparison would hold the task and time budget constant.

01 / Delegation · Connection puzzle

No Signal

A laptop with a black screen. A desk full of cables. Eight levels that ask you to figure out why nothing works.

Play No Signal {{arrow}}
My role
Wrote the design brief for rules, progression, and equipment, drawing on my own desk setup.
Codex’s role
Implemented the brief, starting with one working power chain.
Turning point
The brief became testable in pieces, from device movement to cable limits and live status feedback.

From sketch to game engine

AC tracing tracks visited endpoints to stop cycles. From powered adapters, the runtime adds reachable hub outputs until no new hub can be powered. Port compatibility and cable reach are checked separately.

02 / Collaboration · Platformer and level editor

Catwalk

Guide a pixel cat home while Mocha, a second cat outside the monitor, interferes with the controls.

Play Catwalk {{arrow}} Route Studio {{arrow}} Playable timeline {{arrow}}
My role
Brought Mocha’s real behavior, judged the prototypes, and authored the final route.
Codex’s role
Proposed and built trials for movement, paw interference, and level structure.
Turning point
After 25 layouts with weak interactions between obstacles, I took over level design. The editor grew into a tool for sharing routes.

From route data to physics

The playtest model clips gap intervals to the world, merges overlaps, and emits the remaining spans as collision geometry. The same route file carries checkpoints and Mocha’s triggers.

createGroundSegments() and buildRuntimePlan()

03 / Direction · Tactical puzzle combat

Two Minds 二心

Place Sun Wukong on a 37-hex board. The traits you pass up return in the hands of his double.

Play Two Minds {{arrow}}
My role
Identified a theme through playtesting. A choice should change what happens later.
Codex’s role
Led concept discovery with questions and short possession and ritual prototypes.
Turning point
Early combat felt detached from ritual. It returned when the False Wukong could fight with the traits the player declined.

Rule sketch / State partition

From choice history to combat

The engine records chosen, discarded, and seen trait IDs. It builds the double from discarded IDs. Movement ranks unoccupied, unblocked neighbors by axial hex distance, with coordinate tie-breaks.

chooseTrait(), buildFalseWukong(), and nextStep()

A field handbook

Decide what
to delegate

Three practical entries adapted from my Vibe Coding for Game Design notes and the games’ development records.

Delegation Specify an observable result

Write a rule, a passing case, and a failing case.

Build one power chain. A compatible cable connects within its reach. Moving beyond that reach disconnects it and updates the screen.
Collaboration Test one uncertainty at a time

Save the question, build, and playtest judgment together.

Keep the route fixed. Vary when Mocha blocks an input. Save both versions so I can compare how fair they feel.
Direction Start with questions and keep the rejections

Keep rejected prototypes as design evidence.

Ask one question about a choice I find interesting. Record what draws me in. Build a small test once we know what we want to learn.
